# Corrections & Versioning

A public record should be auditable after publication, not only while it is being written.

## Correction principles

The Citizen's Record should:

- correct material errors promptly;
- identify what changed when the change matters to understanding the record;
- preserve the underlying source trail;
- distinguish a correction from a later development or changed legal status; and
- never silently convert an allegation into a fact or a claim into an outcome.

## When new evidence appears

New information does not automatically invalidate the earlier record. First determine whether it:

1. confirms an existing fact;
2. contradicts an existing fact;
3. adds context without changing the conclusion;
4. changes the status of an allegation or proceeding; or
5. creates a new unresolved question.

Document the relationship between the old and new information.

## Correction record

For significant corrections, record:

- the affected page or record;
- the date of correction;
- the material that was changed;
- the reason for the change;
- the source supporting the correction; and
- any remaining uncertainty.

## What we do not do

We do not delete inconvenient facts merely because they complicate a narrative. We do not treat a correction as proof that every other part of a record was wrong. We do not conceal uncertainty when the evidence does not support certainty.
