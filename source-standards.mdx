# Source Standards

The Citizen's Record is source-first. A strong record is not merely persuasive; it is traceable.

## Source hierarchy

When practical, prefer sources in this order:

1. **Primary official records** — orders, filings, dockets, agency records, statutes, regulations, datasets, and other records created or maintained by the responsible institution.
2. **Direct evidence** — recordings, photographs, original documents, contemporaneous communications, or other material directly connected to an event.
3. **Reliable secondary sources** — reporting or analysis that identifies its underlying sources and can be independently checked.
4. **Claims and commentary** — useful leads, but not substitutes for verification.

## Provenance

For each important source, preserve enough context to answer:

- Who created or published it?
- When was it created or published?
- What is the document or record identifier?
- Where was it obtained?
- Is it complete or partial?
- Has it been superseded, amended, or corrected?

## Status labels

A source's existence does not automatically establish the truth of everything it says. Records should be classified according to what they establish.

- **Official record** — a record issued or maintained by an identified institution or custodian.
- **Verified fact** — information supported by sufficient reliable evidence.
- **Documented allegation** — an allegation whose existence and source can be established.
- **Claim** — an assertion that has not yet been independently established.
- **Unverified information** — information retained as a lead or context but not established as fact.
- **Analysis** — an interpretation of documented material.
- **Outcome** — the documented result of a proceeding or process.
- **Unresolved question** — an issue for which the available record does not yet provide a reliable answer.

## Conflicting sources

When credible sources conflict, preserve the conflict. Do not silently select the version that best fits a preferred narrative. Identify the disagreement, compare provenance, and explain what remains uncertain.

## Corrections

If a source is later corrected, superseded, or shown to be unreliable, update the record while preserving the historical trail. See the project's methodology for the correction standard.
