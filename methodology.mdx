# Methodology

The Citizen's Record is organized around a simple rule: readers should be able to distinguish the evidence from the interpretation.

## Source hierarchy

We prioritize, where available:

1. Original public records and primary documents
2. Official government publications and datasets
3. Court filings, orders, and docket entries
4. Direct statements from identified participants
5. Credible reporting that identifies its sourcing
6. Secondary commentary, clearly labeled as such

## Labels matter

A filing is not a finding. An allegation is not proof. A dismissal is not necessarily an exoneration. A settlement is not necessarily an admission.

We label the status of material whenever practical.

## Corrections

If we get something materially wrong, the goal is to correct the record rather than quietly rewrite history. Significant corrections should explain what changed.

## Uncertainty

Where the available evidence does not establish an answer, we say so. Unknown is a legitimate status.
